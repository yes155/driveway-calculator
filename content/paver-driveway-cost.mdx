## What changes paver driveway cost by material?

**The selected paver product and laying pattern change material and installation work.**

HomeGuide reports concrete pavers at $10–$20, brick at $10–$25 and stone at $10–$30 per square foot installed. NT Pavers publishes a premium natural stone driveway range of $25–$70, including custom-order materials. These different scopes are shown separately. The selected estimate does not average guides or combine their endpoints.

## What belongs beneath the pavers?

**A stable structural base supports the bedding layer and paving units.**

CMHA identifies a 6-inch minimum compacted aggregate base for residential driveways under ideal conditions. Weak soil, poor drainage and freezing conditions require a different structural design. Base depth is a specification to confirm, not an automatic nationwide requirement. Unilock describes a 1-inch bedding course unless an engineer specifies otherwise; suitable bedding sand depends on the chosen system.

## Which details belong in the installation quote?

**The quote identifies excavation, base work, edge restraint, joint sand and cleanup.**

Ask whether ordinary base preparation and material delivery are already included in the installed rate. Add only separate work actually needed; removal, drainage correction and elaborate cutting require their own descriptions. Specify vehicle-rated pavers instead of assuming a patio product supports driveway traffic.

## How do maintenance and ordering affect the budget?

**Pavers require cleaning, joint inspection and repairs to affected units or support layers.**

Selected concrete paver guidance gives 25–50 years with proper installation and maintenance, not a guarantee for every stone product. Sealers and joint materials follow manufacturer instructions. Use the paver calculator to separate unit count, base material and bedding sand before ordering. The driveway comparison tool compares upfront surface costs; an unsourced annual upkeep dollar figure stays absent.
