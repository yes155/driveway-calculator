## How do you calculate crusher run for a base?

**Calculate area × depth in feet, divide by 27, then multiply by the supplier’s tons-per-yard figure.**

Enter the depth of the crusher run base layer itself. Bedding sand, paving blocks and a clean surface topping belong to separate calculations. Measure a tapered or irregular footprint in smaller sections so the base quantity reflects the actual area.

## What density does crusher run use?

**The density belongs to the supplier’s grading, rock source and bulk condition.**

Triple R Dirt Works lists its 1.5-inch crusher run at 2,450 lb per cubic yard, equivalent to 1.225 US short tons. L&S describes a 2,500–3,000 lb range and a 1.4 planning conversion. American Rock Supply uses about 3,000 lb, or 1.5 tons per yard. The substantial difference stays visible. The editable preset names the Triple R product rather than claiming one universal crusher-run value.

## How does compaction affect the base order?

**A supplier-confirmed loose-to-finished allowance adjusts the volume before purchase rounding.**

The entered allowance needs to match the volume condition of the density. Do not apply a loose-volume increase to a conversion already specified for compacted material. Zero excludes the adjustment; it does not describe the compactability of crusher run. Waste and compaction represent different causes of extra material and need separate justification.

## What needs checking before the truck arrives?

**Confirm material grade, finished depth, purchase increment, truck payload and access.**

The calculator estimates tons; it does not prescribe base thickness from soil and loading conditions. Ask the supplier whether its conversion refers to stockpile or compacted material and whether moisture is included.
