## What is measured before choosing a filler?

**Measure the crack’s length, width, and depth, and inspect its surroundings.**

A long narrow crack differs from a pothole or interconnected failure. Record the surface material and any old repair product present. A label for asphalt does not establish compatibility with concrete. The guide provides selection criteria; it does not claim a tested best product or a universally correct filler for all driveway damage.

## How do cold pour and hot pour differ in the buying decision?

**They describe different application systems with product-specific equipment and instructions.**

A cold pour example is Latex-ite 2X, labeled for asphalt cracks up to 1/2 inch wide. Hot pour selection requires the exact sealant’s heating and equipment instructions; no heating temperature is supplied without that documentation. Keep a contractor’s sealant specification separate from a homeowner’s ready-to-apply bottle. Neither application form fixes sinking or an unstable base.

## How much coverage does one container provide?

**Coverage depends on the product and the crack’s cross-section.**

SealMaster Acrylic Crack Filler lists approximately 75–150 linear feet per US gallon depending on width and depth, without a reference cross-section. Treat that as a product range rather than guaranteed yield. Latex-ite 2X’s reviewed page supplies no verified linear-foot yield. Do not replace missing gallon coverage with an area-depth figure from a powder patch product.

## What matters during application and sealing afterward?

**Cleaning, label weather limits, and cure time control the repair procedure.**

SealMaster Acrylic Crack Filler is used without dilution and opens to traffic after drying. The selected product label controls any delay before sealcoating. Compare 3 instructions: preparation, permitted crack dimensions, and traffic access. The repair cost guide prices professional crack filling by linear foot, while the sealcoating guide prices a full-area maintenance service. Connected cracking, recurring failures, and standing water require a broader assessment before another application of filler.
