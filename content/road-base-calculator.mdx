## How do you calculate a road base quantity?

**Convert layer depth to feet, multiply by area and divide by 27 for cubic yards.**

Then multiply cubic yards by the selected US short tons per cubic yard. The base layer is one part of the pavement structure; do not include asphalt, concrete, pavers or bedding sand in its thickness. Divide irregular areas into measured sections before adding their quantities.

## Which density belongs to road base?

**Match the conversion to the supplier’s exact base product.**

Sutherland lists AB-minus crushed road base at 2,860 lb per cubic yard, equivalent to 1.43 US short tons. Lyngso lists virgin Class 2 base at 1.5 tons per yard. Rock N Yard lists its recycled 1.5-inch-minus base at about 1.3. These grades and sources differ. The calculator’s named Sutherland preset stays editable, with the alternatives visible in the reference table.

## What does the compaction input mean?

**It records an entered adjustment between the measured finished layer and loose delivered volume.**

Confirm that relationship with the supplier for the material on the order. Moisture affects stockpile weight, and grading affects packing. The current Rock N Yard page gives no numerical universal shrinkage factor, so an older search snippet is not a valid default. Zero compaction allowance excludes an adjustment and makes no recommendation about base construction.

## What needs checking before ordering road base?

**Check the project’s material specification, depth condition and supplier sale unit.**

Confirm tons or cubic yards, delivery minimum, purchase increment and truck capacity. A material estimate does not establish structural depth or drainage design.
