## What changes tar and chip driveway cost?

**The existing base, aggregate selection and number of chip-seal applications affect the work.**

Angi reports a wider overall range of $1–$5 per square foot. The Spruce reports $4–$8.35 for a new chip-seal driveway in its 2024 guide. Those conflicting benchmarks remain visible rather than becoming an average. HomeGuide’s $1–$3 resurfacing range is a different operation and is not used as the new-installation default.

## How is the surface installed?

**The crew places binder and stone in a coordinated sequence, then uses rolling to seat the chips.**

The support beneath the surface needs assessment before application. A defective base or unresolved drainage problem remains a problem beneath a new wearing layer. Ask the installer to identify the preparation, selected aggregate chips, number of layers and traffic-opening instructions in writing.

## What lifespan does the research support?

**HomeGuide, Uniform Paving and JR Paving report a 7–10-year driveway lifespan range.**

The contractor examples cover Georgia and Western Pennsylvania, with maintenance and traffic conditions attached. Treat the range as a planning interval to substantial surface renewal, not the permanent life of the underlying base. Angi reports a 10-year average; that single value does not replace the selected range.

## What maintenance and quote checks matter?

**Remove loose chips and debris, maintain drainage, and ask the installer how local damage is treated.**

The research does not establish a complete national annual upkeep cost. Confirm removal, base corrections and hauling rather than assuming they are included. Compare this scope with the asphalt driveway cost guide to understand the difference between site-applied chip seal and hot mix paving. Surface texture, vehicle use and site access belong in that comparison, alongside the estimate.
