## What does the material list represent?

**Each row is a sourced product or material condition, not one weight for every material with the same name.**

Asphalt density refers to an in-place mixture. Aggregate supplier conversions refer to bulk material with voids and varying moisture. Normal-weight concrete references describe concrete material rather than a truckload of loose demolition debris. Match that condition before using the chart to calculate weight.

## What is asphalt weight per cubic foot?

**The Asphalt Institute gives 142–148 pounds per cubic foot for an in-place asphalt mixture and uses 148 for quantity estimates.**

The weight of asphalt depends on its aggregate, binder and achieved compaction. Craig Paving uses a 148 lb-per-cubic-foot estimating value; Minnesota’s association uses a compacted spread factor. These assumptions stay named and editable instead of being averaged into an unexplained default.

## How much does a yard of asphalt weigh?

**Multiply the selected in-place asphalt density by 27 cubic feet to obtain pounds per cubic yard.**

Divide that weight by 2,000 to convert to US short tons. The calculator-generated conversion table shows the arithmetic for the selected source. A loose truck volume is not automatically the same as the completed compacted volume.

## Why do gravel and concrete weights differ?

**Different material structures and moisture conditions give different bulk or in-place densities.**

Two Rivers lists separate weights for crushed rock, pea gravel and washed round rock. Sutherland, Lyngso and Rock N Yard describe different road-base products. The concrete reference uses the named normal-weight basis, with an editable density. A universal gravel number hides these distinctions. Use the [Gravel Weight per Cubic Yard](/gravel-weight-per-cubic-yard/) for supplier comparisons and the [Cubic Yards to Tons](/cubic-yards-to-tons/) for a known volume.
