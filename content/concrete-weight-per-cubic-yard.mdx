## How much does a yard of concrete weigh?

**Concrete weight per cubic yard equals density multiplied by the cubic feet in a yard.**

ACI’s retrieved concrete terminology gives normal-weight concrete an approximate density of 150 pounds per cubic foot. Multiplying that reference by 27 cubic feet gives 4,050 pounds per cubic yard. NRMCA lists normal-weight concrete at 140–150 pounds per cubic foot; FHWA’s archived engineering classification spans 135–155. These are compatible reference scopes, not identical universal densities. Keep the density editable.

## How much does a cubic yard of concrete weigh by mix?

**The mix density determines the weight, rather than the volume label alone.**

NRMCA lists structural lightweight concrete at 90–120 pounds per cubic foot, distinct from the normal-weight reference. The calculator-generated conversion supplies pounds per cubic yard and short tons per cubic yard for the selected density. Ask the producer for the mix-specific density when material weight affects transport, equipment, or loading. Do not substitute the reference weight for a structural assessment.

## How much does a yard of cured concrete weigh?

**A cured concrete weight requires a density stated for the material’s condition.**

Moisture condition, mix composition, and aggregate type affect density. The research does not establish a single separate cured-concrete or dry-concrete density for every driveway mix. “How much does a yard of dry concrete weigh?” also describes bagged dry mix in some searches; dry bag mass is not the finished wet yield. The [Concrete Calculator](/concrete-calculator/) uses a named bag product’s stated yield for bag counts.

## How much does a yard of crushed concrete weigh?

**Crushed concrete needs its supplier’s bulk density, rather than the density of an intact slab.**

Loose crushed material contains void space and belongs to a different material condition. No compatible crushed-concrete density is selected in this concrete dataset. Enter the supplier’s weight per measured volume instead of carrying over 150 pounds per cubic foot. The [Material Weight Chart](/material-weights/) identifies material and density assumptions, and the [Cubic Yards to Tons](/cubic-yards-to-tons/) applies the selected value. Before using a weight result, confirm the mix or material, moisture condition, density units, and transport limits.
