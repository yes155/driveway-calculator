## Is every driveway built to the same thickness?

**Asphalt driveway thickness depends on the design, traffic load, and supporting ground.**

CAPA’s technical brief distinguishes asphalt over an aggregate base from full-depth asphalt construction. Harrison Paving and Nathan’s Paving describe regional passenger-car designs using 2–3 inches of compacted asphalt over a granular base. These are named design examples, not one nationwide requirement. Confirm the planned section with the contractor before estimating tons.

## How does base thickness differ from asphalt thickness?

**Base course and asphalt layers are separate parts of the pavement section.**

CAPA’s example uses a 4-inch aggregate base for fair or good subgrade and a 6-inch base for poor subgrade. Do not enter the combined pavement depth as the asphalt thickness. Record the base material separately from the asphalt mix. The subgrade is the supporting ground beneath those layers; weak areas and drainage concerns require attention in the project design.

## Does the calculator use loose or compacted depth?

**Use final compacted asphalt depth with the calculator’s in-place density assumption.**

The Asphalt Institute publishes an estimated mixture density range of 142–148 pounds per cubic foot. Its named 148-pound value is editable. Combining final volume with in-place density already describes compacted material; another automatic compaction multiplier counts that adjustment twice. A supplier’s order allowance is a separate explicit input, not a substitute for specifying the pavement section.

## What belongs in a paving quote?

**The quote needs the asphalt section, base section, and preparation scope.**

Check 3 details: the thickness after compaction, the material and depth beneath it, and the treatment of soft or poorly drained areas. A paving price without those details is difficult to compare. The asphalt calculator turns specified thickness and measurements into tons. The asphalt driveway cost guide separates that material quantity from installed pricing. Keep project drawings and contractor recommendations with the saved estimate so the assumptions remain visible.
