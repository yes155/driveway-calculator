## What belongs in a routine inspection?

**Look for new cracks, loose edges, surface failures, and blocked drainage.**

Blacktop Concepts recommends attending to cracking, standing water, spills, and localized deterioration. Photograph changes so repeat problems are easier to identify. Edging keeps the boundary visible for inspection; it does not repair a weak base. Check where runoff leaves the driveway and record recurring puddles for the contractor rather than covering them with sealer.

## When does crack filling enter the schedule?

**Assess cracks when they appear instead of waiting for a whole-driveway maintenance appointment.**

Measure length and width, and distinguish an isolated line from interconnected alligator cracking. Angi’s crack-filling benchmark is $1–$3 per linear foot, with minimum charges affecting small jobs. The crack filler guide explains label compatibility. The repair guide distinguishes that linear measurement from patch area and pothole project totals, so every maintenance item retains its own pricing unit.

## How is a sealing schedule chosen?

**Sealing follows the selected product instructions and the driveway’s wear and condition.**

Published guide intervals conflict; no universal schedule is asserted. Henry HE200’s linked technical sheet requires at least 6 months before coating new asphalt, but that is a named product requirement. Confirm the current label before buying. The sealcoating cost guide covers professional application, and the sealer guide explains products without turning a warranty label into proof of service life.

## What is the annual upkeep cost?

**The reviewed sources do not establish a complete annual maintenance cost.**

A sealing application price covers one service, not every year’s inspection, cleaning, crack filling, patching, or drainage work. Do not divide a guide price by an arbitrary interval and present it as sourced annual upkeep. Keep a maintenance record with three details: the material used, the treatment date, and the repaired area. A recurrence in the same location is useful information for the next contractor assessment. Compare matched scopes when planning maintenance expenditure.
