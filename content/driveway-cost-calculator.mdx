## How does the driveway cost calculator compare surfaces?

**One set of measurements produces a separate installed range for each selected surface.**

The driveway calculator supports asphalt, standard concrete, basic stamped concrete, gravel, pavers, and tar and chip. It preserves the named publisher’s work scope. The driveway cost estimator uses the same area for every row, then sorts by upfront cost or upfront cost per year of the recorded service-life range.

## What does cost per year of life mean?

**This figure divides upfront cost by the stated service-life range; it excludes maintenance and financing.**

The low annualized value uses low cost divided by the high lifespan; the high value uses high cost divided by the low lifespan. A missing lifespan hides this value without hiding a known upfront estimate. Gravel has no comparable finite renewal-life range, and basic stamped concrete lacks a separately verified range. Annual upkeep totals remain unavailable; inspection, cleaning, repair, and surface renewal are separate tasks.

## Which costs belong outside the surface range?

**Only separately quoted work excluded from installation belongs in the extras.**

Avoid counting removal, ordinary base preparation, and delivery twice. Compare equivalent excavation, disposal, reinforcement, stone base, finish, and access conditions. Supplier hauling and short-load charges depend on the actual order. Low, average, and high regional multipliers stay unavailable; baseline means no index adjustment.

## How do I keep a comparison for my project?

**Copy, print, and share controls retain the displayed result and its input assumptions.**

Select the surface of interest before saving a comparison. Reset restores the initial reference state. A quote request remains disabled until the approved lead-network configuration exists. The comparison is an estimate, not a contractor quote or a promise of service life.
