## How do you calculate asphalt millings?

**Volume equals area × depth in feet, and tons equal cubic yards × the selected tons-per-yard factor.**

Divide cubic feet by 27 for cubic yards. Keep the finished layer depth separate from the loose quantity delivered. Reclaimed asphalt pavement retains asphalt binder and aggregate, but its delivered stockpile weight is different from the in-place density of fresh hot mix.

## Which density belongs to millings?

**Use the supplier’s factor for the delivered material and its stated condition.**

Anchorage Sand & Gravel lists 1-inch-minus RAP at about 2,800 lb per cubic yard, or 1.4 US short tons. It identifies these as stockpile weights that vary with moisture, texture and gradation. Lasalle lists millings and screened millings at 2,500 lb per yard, equivalent to 1.25 tons. Hello Gravel uses 1.4 as its planning conversion. The calculator’s named reference is editable; the difference between suppliers stays visible.

## How does compaction change the order?

**Compaction changes the volume relationship, so use a documented allowance for the actual product.**

A stockpile conversion applied to a compacted finished volume needs the supplier’s loose-to-finished adjustment. Do not add the same allowance twice. Zero excludes an extra adjustment; it is not a recommendation for uncompacted construction. FHWA publishes separate processed-RAP and compacted-density observations. Those conditions do not form one interchangeable default.

## What needs checking before delivery?

**Confirm gradation, depth condition, purchase increment, truck capacity and the drop location.**

Ask whether the supplier sells screened or unscreened material and whether its quote is per ton or per yard. Use the [Asphalt Millings Cost](/asphalt-millings-cost/) for driveway installation scope.
