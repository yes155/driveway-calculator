## What gets prepared before the paving crew arrives?

**The supporting ground, base course, and drainage need a defined preparation scope.**

Blacktop Concepts describes removal of unsuitable material, correction of soft areas, and compaction before paving. Identify the final levels at the garage, street connection, and edges. Record whether excavation and disposal belong to the paving quote or a separate preparatory contract. Existing water problems belong in that scope before another surface is placed.

## How are thickness and tons established?

**The project section establishes asphalt depth; measured dimensions establish the area.**

CAPA’s residential example has 3 inches of asphalt over an aggregate base, while other construction sections differ. Measure the driveway’s shape and identify tapered or irregular sections. The asphalt calculator converts the specified compacted section into tons using an editable named density. A saved quantity estimate does not replace a pavement design or a supplier’s mix recommendation.

## Why are temperature and rolling important?

**Hot mix needs placement and compaction while it remains workable.**

FHWA describes traditional hot-mix production temperatures of 300–350°F; this is plant mixing information, not a universal field paving target. The producer’s mix instructions and the crew’s construction plan govern placement and rolling. Separate the paving temperature from weather limits on later sealing. Cooling, curing, and vehicle access are different subjects, so obtain the crew’s written opening instructions.

## What belongs in the DIY versus hire comparison?

**Compare the complete job scope rather than material cost alone.**

Angi’s new-asphalt benchmark is $7–$13 per square foot including installation without major grading. A DIY purchase of material excludes transport, placing equipment, rolling, and corrective work. No verified universal DIY total is supplied here. Compare 3 responsibilities: preparation, material delivery and placement, and finished compaction. The cost guide explains pricing assumptions; the thickness guide distinguishes layers. Keep paving equipment operation with the planned qualified crew and confirm access before delivery.
