## What goes into the mixture?

**The aggregate provides the mineral structure and the asphalt binder holds the mixture together.**

FHWA describes asphalt pavement mixture as approximately 90–95% aggregate by weight. That is a composition description rather than a recipe for a homeowner to reproduce. The National Asphalt Pavement Association distinguishes mixture types and production methods. Specify the producer’s named mix for the driveway instead of treating every asphalt product as interchangeable.

## What does hot mix mean?

**The term describes a heated production and construction process.**

FHWA’s discussion places traditional HMA mixing temperature at 300–350°F. The producer’s mix instructions govern its actual production and field requirements. Warm mix uses a different temperature process, and packaged cold patch has different application instructions. Plant mixing temperature is not a driveway surface temperature or a weather threshold for applying sealer.

## Which density belongs in a tonnage estimate?

**Use a named mixture estimating density or the producer’s project-specific value.**

The Asphalt Institute gives an in-place range of 142–148 pounds per cubic foot and uses 148 in its example. That value is an editable planning assumption. Bulk aggregate, reclaimed millings, and a compacted asphalt mixture describe different material conditions. Substituting one for another changes an order estimate without changing the driveway’s measured dimensions.

## How does the mixture become a driveway?

**The crew places the workable mix over the specified support and compacts it to the planned section.**

Thickness, supporting layers, and drainage form part of that construction scope. Use the asphalt calculator for geometric volume and tonnage after selecting the compacted section. The material weights page compares named density assumptions. Do not add an automatic compaction percentage when final compacted depth and in-place density are already used. Keep supplier product, temperature instructions, and order quantity with the quote so the same assumptions are available to the paving crew.

Mix temperature affects workability and compaction. The producer’s mix requirements govern placement conditions.
