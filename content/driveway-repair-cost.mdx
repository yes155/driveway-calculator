## Which driveway repair prices match the defect?

**Match the unit price to the measured repair and its method.**

Angi lists asphalt crack filling at $1–$3 per linear foot; HomeGuide lists hot patching at $2–$5 per square foot and pothole repair at $100–$400 per pothole. These prices describe different operations. A per-pothole price is not converted into a per-area rate without a defined repair size.

## What changes concrete repair costs?

**Crack treatment, slab movement and support problems require separate scopes.**

The selected minor concrete crack-repair benchmark is $0.50–$3 per linear foot from HomeGuide and Angi. A wider structural crack or settling slab is not the same service. Describe movement, uneven edges and water collection to the contractor. Product label limits determine whether a filler suits the measured crack; a surface filler does not stabilize moving soil.

## When does resurfacing differ from patching?

**Resurfacing treats a broader wearing surface over suitable support.**

The asphalt overlay guide uses Angi’s $3–$7 per square foot range, while methods involving removal of the old top layer have separate prices. Confirm the preparation and finished thickness. A new surface does not remove the cause of reappearing cracks or persistent drainage damage.

## How are gravel problems repaired?

**Regrading and replenishing material restore the shape and surface where those operations address the defect.**

Document potholes, rutting, displaced stone and the runoff path. Clear obstructed drainage before covering the area with fresh gravel. No universal gravel repair depth or annual budget is assumed. Use the material calculator for each specified layer, then obtain separate quotes for hauling, material and equipment.

## When is replacement the next comparison?

**Replacement belongs in the discussion when the damaged surface or support needs removal and rebuilding.**

Use the asphalt and concrete repair guides for method-specific detail. The replacement guide compares demolition, disposal, subbase assessment and the new surface. The contractor’s inspection establishes the repair scope; a price table alone does not diagnose pavement failure.
