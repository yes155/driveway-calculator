## What are you buying when you order millings?

**Asphalt millings are reclaimed asphalt pavement used as a granular material.**

The material comes from processed existing pavement rather than a fresh hot-mix production run. Product size, screening, and condition affect the order. Ask the supplier to identify the grade and whether its ton means a US short ton. A material quote does not automatically include spreading, base preparation, roller compaction, or delivery to your driveway.

## How do supplier prices compare?

**Each published supplier price describes a local product and pickup arrangement.**

UCS in Milltown, Wisconsin, lists recycled asphalt at $15 per ton FOB, excluding tax. Sargent’s Hermon, Maine, list has reclaim/recycled HMA at $20 per ton FOB. Yarberry in Anderson, Indiana, lists millings at $23 per ton without stated delivery inclusion. These examples are not combined into a national delivered price, and availability requires supplier confirmation.

## What changes the installed driveway price?

**Preparation, hauling, and compaction distinguish an installed quote from a material purchase.**

HomeGuide’s $2–$5 benchmark differs from Angi’s $3–$8 and Fragale’s Connecticut $1–$3 example. Compare three inclusions: the prepared base, delivery to the site, and finished compaction. Ask how transitions and runoff are handled. A cheap stockpile is not a finished driveway, and a thick loose layer is not the same measurement as final compacted depth.

## How much material does your driveway need?

**Use the millings calculator with the measured area, depth, and an identified bulk density.**

The Anchorage supplier preset is a named ordering assumption, not a universal density. Keep any supplier compaction allowance explicit so it is not added twice. The gravel driveway cost guide provides the adjacent loose-surface comparison. Upkeep includes watching for rutting and drainage problems; no complete annual maintenance price is established by the reviewed sources. Obtain a site-specific quote before treating any guide range as a contract price.

Material quotes use US short tons. Base depth and drainage condition affect the required construction scope; millings tons alone do not price grading and compaction.
