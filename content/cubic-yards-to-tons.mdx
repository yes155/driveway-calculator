## How do you convert cubic yards to tons?

**Tons = cubic yards × US short tons per cubic yard.**

A cubic yard is volume and a short ton is mass, so density connects the units. When a supplier gives pounds per cubic yard, divide its figure by 2,000 before using a tons-per-yard input. A metric tonne is a different mass unit; this converter uses US short tons.

## How many tons are in a cubic yard of gravel?

**The number equals the actual product’s bulk density in tons per cubic yard.**

Two Rivers lists its 5/8-inch crushed rock at about 1.4 and its 1/2-inch washed pea gravel at about 1.3. Sutherland’s AB-minus road base is 1.43. These named references describe different materials. Use the supplier’s product conversion when it is available, and retain moisture or compaction conditions on the estimate.

## How many yards are in a ton of gravel?

**Divide one US short ton by the selected tons-per-cubic-yard figure.**

This inverse formula answers a known-tonnage question without assuming that every ton fills one yard. The generated result displays the volume for the exact density entered. Use the same material type and condition in both directions; switching density mid-conversion changes the answer.

## Does the converter include compaction and delivery?

**The conversion alone changes units and does not choose a finished depth or delivery allowance.**

Use the [Gravel Calculator](/gravel-calculator/) to turn dimensions and depth into a material order. Use the [Material Weight Chart](/material-weights/) to compare named density references. A converter result does not establish a truck’s permitted payload or a project’s structural specification.
