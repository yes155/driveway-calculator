2. Foundation Brief

Source Context. A US-facing set of free driveway cost guides and
material calculators. Revenue comes from (1) lead or call network
payouts when visitors request contractor quotes, (2) display ads on tool
pages, and (3) affiliate commissions on repair and sealing products.

Central Entity. Driveway

Brand and domain. Driveway Calculators at drivewaycalc.online. Tagline:
Driveway cost guides and material calculators. Author: Farrukh Abdullah,
researcher and writer. Write the brand in full as Driveway Calculators.
It appears in the home page title, the schema, the footer and the lead
disclosure text only, so the other title tags stay under 60 characters.

Ontology (summary).

• Surfaces: asphalt, concrete (plain and stamped), gravel, pavers, tar
and chip, asphalt millings

• Project types: new installation, replacement, resurfacing or overlay,
repair, sealcoating

• Cost drivers: area in square feet, thickness in inches, base
preparation, removal, labor, delivery, regional price level

• Quantities: tons, cubic yards, square feet, gallons

• Decisions: concrete vs asphalt, resurface vs replace, DIY vs hire

• Supporting materials: gravel, rock, pea gravel, river rock, crusher
run, road base (bridge to Driveway through gravel driveways)

Central Search Intent. Working out how much a driveway project costs and
how much material it needs, so a US homeowner can budget, order
materials or request contractor quotes.

Buyer personas.

1. Homeowner planning a driveway project: highest lead value, arrives on
cost and estimator pages.

2. DIY builder or small contractor estimating material: arrives on
calculators, produces ad revenue and links.

3. Landscaper or homeowner buying aggregate: arrives on gravel and rock
calculators, produces ad revenue and feeds gravel driveway pages.

Core Section. Cost guides, cost estimators and the driveway quantity
calculators (asphalt, gravel driveway, concrete driveway). Lead payouts
and the highest CPCs (1.76 to 3.83 USD on cost terms) sit here.

Outer Section. Aggregate calculators, converters, a material weight
reference, repair product guides and a methodology page. They build
Historical Data and pass relevance into Core pages as neighborhood
content.

Topical border (out of scope). Near me, contractor, company and
directory queries; city and state pages; the Asphalt 8 video game and
other unrelated senses of asphalt; general construction math unrelated
to driveways. Concrete and paver calculators (Phase 3) stay scoped to
driveways and flat slabs.

Balance Law note. Aggregate calculators are Medium relevance to
Driveway. Keep their copy on quantity, and add one bridging section per
page that links to the matching driveway cost page. Do not let
landscaping content spread into the cost pages.

Authority sources. Primary: asphaltisbest.com. Its asphalt calculator
holds #1 for asphalt calculator and figuring asphalt with roughly 6 to 9
referring domains and 240+ ranking keywords on one URL. Study its
single-page tool structure, not its content. Others: pikeindustries.com
(calculator with a quote request), concretenetwork.com (754 ranking
keywords on one concrete driveway cost page), homewyse.com
(estimator-style cost pages), and calculator.net, inchcalculator.com and
omnicalculator.com as gravel calculator benchmarks that hold the top
three and are not realistic targets.

3. Architecture, tech and monetization modules

URL rules. Flat, one level, lowercase, hyphenated, trailing slash. No
dates and no categories in URLs. Navigation groups: Costs, Calculators,
Materials, Repair and Maintenance, About.

Page types and templates.

• Cost guide: answer block, range table, cost factors, quick estimate,
Q&A

• Cost estimator (tool): inputs, low-to-high result, assumptions, quote
CTA

• Quantity calculator (tool): inputs, result, formula, density table

• Converter or reference: input, result, lookup table

• Comparison: side-by-side table, decision rules

• Product guide (affiliate): criteria, picks, how to use

• Hub or pillar: surface comparison, links to every child page

Modules on every cost and tool page, in this order.

1. Answer block: one declarative sentence with the number or result

2. The calculator or quick estimator

3. Result block with a Get free quotes CTA (ZIP, project type)

4. How it is calculated: formula, default values, editable fields

5. Q&A sections, each starting with the direct answer

6. Related pages, each link with annotation text stating the
relationship

7. Sources named in text, last updated date, author

8. Disclosure line for leads and affiliate links

Recommended stack. Next.js (App Router) with TypeScript, Tailwind and
MDX; calculators as pure TypeScript functions with unit tests; static
generation. Host on Cloudflare Pages or Vercel Pro (Vercel's free Hobby
plan is for non-commercial use, so check current terms). The lead
endpoint runs as a Cloudflare Pages Function with D1 storage; on Vercel
Pro use a Next.js route handler instead. Analytics: GA4 and Search
Console. Reserve ad slot heights to prevent layout shift. Schema:
Organization, BreadcrumbList, WebApplication for tools, Article with
dateModified. Google limits FAQ rich results to a small set of sites, so
keep Q&A visible but do not plan around a rich result.

Monetization.

• Leads: build the CTA module now, but switch it on only after a lead or
call network approves you and supplies its consent language and routing
details (see 8.5).

• Ads: apply to AdSense once 15 to 20 pages are live and check
eligibility for your country; move to a premium ad network when its
traffic threshold is met (verify current thresholds).

• Affiliate: repair and sealing product guides (Section 6, Seq 31 and
32).

Trust. Real author name and bio, a methodology page, sources named in
text, a visible last-updated date. Do not invent a US address, reviewer
or contractor credential.

10.2 Compare-surfaces estimator spec (Seq 42)

One set of measurements returns a low-to-high cost for each surface side
by side. It feeds the comparison and pillar pages and is the strongest
place for the quote CTA.

Inputs. Area (several sections allowed), cost area tier (low, average,
high), extras (remove old surface, base preparation, delivery), surfaces
to compare (asphalt, concrete, stamped concrete, gravel, pavers, tar and
chip), and one radio button for the surface the visitor is most
interested in.

Outputs per surface. Low and high total, lifespan range in years, annual
upkeep items, and cost per year of life (low and high). The default sort
is lowest upfront cost, with a toggle to sort by cost per year.

Total for surface s, low and high:

T_s = A \cdot u_s \cdot t + e_s

Cost per year of life:

C_s = \frac{T_s}{L_s}

A is area in square feet, u_s is the unit price per square foot from
/data/prices.json (low and high), t is the cost area tier multiplier,
e_s is the extras for that surface, and L_s is the lifespan in years
from /data/prices.json.

Rules.

• Show a range for every surface, never a single price, and label every
result as an estimate, not a quote.

• Cost per year stays hidden for any surface whose lifespan is null.

• Show the assumptions under the table: tier, extras included, and the
source name and date for each price.

• The quote CTA prefills the radio-button surface and the area from the
estimator.

• Share link: encode the inputs in the URL query string so a result can
be sent to someone else. Add a print stylesheet so the result prints as
a one-page summary.

• The SERPs differ: driveway paving cost calculator matches the asphalt
estimator SERP, so that term stays on Seq 4 and this page keeps the
surface-agnostic terms.

Data to add to the price sheet in 7.1. Lifespan range in years per
surface, annual upkeep cost per surface, and which extras apply to which
surface. Record the source name and retrieval date for each, and leave
each value null until sourced.

Shape and slope formulas. A tapered driveway has start width w1 and end
width w2. If the visitor measures the length along the slope, use it as
entered. If the visitor measures a flat distance and gives a grade,
adjust the length.

A = L \cdot \frac{w_1 + w_2}{2}

L_s = L_f \cdot \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{g}{100}\right)^2}

L is length, L_f is the flat distance, L_s is the length along the slope
and g is the grade in percent.

Delivery logic. Loads equal tons divided by truck capacity, rounded up.
A short-load fee applies when a concrete order is below the supplier
minimum in cubic yards. Every fee and minimum comes from
/data/prices.json and stays null until sourced.

Worked examples. Generate every worked example by calling the calculator
function with the example inputs, and add a test that fails if the page
text differs from the function output. One competitor example lists 155
bags of 80 lb for about 5.2 cubic yards, which looks about 40 percent
low if concrete weighs about 4,050 lb per cubic yard (the figure on
Concrete Tools).

Density and citations. Omni assumes 1.25 metric tonnes per cubic meter

10.7 Keyword coverage rules

Every page uses the exact words of each keyword in its row, once or
twice, in a natural place. Google matches meaning as well as words, so
this helps clarity, snippet matching and long-tail reach, and it does
not guarantee rankings.

Where each keyword goes

• Primary keyword: title tag, H1, first sentence, one H2 and the meta
description.

• Fold-in keywords: question headings, table row labels, worked
examples, the Before you order checklist, image alt text and the
sentences around sibling links.

• Question keywords become question headings, and the sentence under
each one answers directly.

• Merged names appear once in a sentence that explains them. On the
asphalt calculator, say that some countries call asphalt bitumen, so the
page also works as a bitumen calculator. On the gravel calculator, say
it also works as a stone calculator and a crushed stone calculator. On
the rock calculator, cover river rock and landscape rock.

Limits

• No exact keyword more than 4 times on a page. This is an editorial
guideline, not a Google rule.

• If an exact keyword is ungrammatical, such as how much for a asphalt
driveway, use a natural close variant and note it in the coverage
report.

• Never write a sentence only to hold a keyword.

• Cement driveway wording follows the Seq 5 rule in 10.3.

Checks. Each page lists its keywords in frontmatter, the build fails
when one is missing, and prompt 8.7 prints a coverage report that the
8.8 audit then checks.
